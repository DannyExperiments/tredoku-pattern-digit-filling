## Findings

- **Original problem.** The wording was verified in the published EJC version (33(3), #P3.58, published 11 September 2026, Section 7, item 2) and in arXiv versions v1–v4. The question was asked in v1 (July 2024). The conjecture "I conjecture the answer is 'yes'" was added in v2 (July 2024). - **Status.** The question is still open in the published version. No erratum or update was found on the EJC page, Blackburn's pages or MathWorld. The only work citing it is Ridout, *Professor Preece's tredoku tilings* (arXiv:2511.12680), which studies which tilings exist and does not construct fillings. No DOI claiming a solution was found in Crossref or DataCite.
- **Identical or stronger results.** None found. Searches covered lozenge and rhombus Latin labellings, sudoku on polyhedra and cube surfaces, gerechte designs, de Bruijn ribbons with labellings, and puzzle patents and solvers. Classification: **apparently new after a documented search through 26 November 2026**, confidence about 0.85.
- **Proof ingredients (all standard).** Runs are pieces of de Bruijn ribbons (also called worms or train tracks), and the no-loop property is standard for tilings (Frettlöh–Harriss 2013). The forms x, y, x+y form a Latin square, that is, three mutually orthogonal partitions. Run offsets are the lozenge analogue of design-key constructions of Sudoku solutions (Patterson–Bailey 1978; Bailey–Cameron–Connelly 2008). Published 2×2 "Tredoku Kids" puzzles exist for particular patterns (q = 2). The paper credits all of these.
